Which rule applies · sources (tap to open)

Conduit is not a manufactured product, so the Oct 1, 2026 rule with its 55 percent test does not apply to it. Galvanized steel rigid metal conduit is an iron or steel product: every manufacturing process, including galvanizing, must be in the United States (23 CFR 635.410(b)). HDPE conduit is a construction material: every manufacturing process, from combining the plastic inputs to the finished conduit, must be in the United States (2 CFR 184.6(a)(2)). The tables list only conduit that a state DOT list flags for Buy America, or lists on condition of a domestic steel certification; the CFR table at the bottom quotes the rules.

  • TX – TxDOT Material Producer List – Roadway Illumination and Electrical Supplies, Item 618 Conduit (September 1, 2026). "Compliant v5" = manufacturer certified through TxDOT's Gold Star Program for projects federally obligated on or after Oct 1, 2026. "Compliant v4" covers Oct 1, 2025 – Sept 30, 2026 only, and "Project Level Certification Required" means not pre-certified; those rows are on the leads page.
  • FL – FDOT Approved Product List – Spec 630 Conduit (read November 15, 2026). Each record prints "BABA Eligible" and FDOT's classification (construction material, iron/steel, manufactured product). FDOT: "Product eligibility (BABA compliance) will be documented through the APL." HDPE listings print a size range, not order codes. FDOT's rigid steel conduit listings are limited to named projects or to lettings through Dec 31, 2025; they are on the leads page.
  • NC – NCDOT ITS and Signals Qualified Products List (workbook) (Feb 2026 list, file dated September 23, 2026). Column "Meets BABA" (Yes / No / blank) with no legend. 2-inch HDPE: Atkore and Blue Diamond are Yes, Dura-Line is No. Every rigid metal conduit row is blank.
  • OH – Ohio DOT Qualified Products List 725.04 – Rigid Metal Conduit (report run November 15, 2026). No flag on the rows; listing requires "A notarized statement stating the steel is domestic and meets the Department's requirements for domestic steel". The polyethylene conduit list (725.052) has no such condition.
  • IA – Iowa DOT MAPLE – Highway Lighting, Rigid Galvanized Steel Conduit (IM 485.10) (read November 15, 2026). No flag on the rows; brand approval requires "Confirmation of compliance to IM 107" (Iowa's Buy America instruction).

CFR references for conduit

Quoted from eCFR (23 CFR 635.410 and 2 CFR part 184, current as of November 12, 2026). Confirm the current text, and any waiver, with FHWA before relying on it.

Applies toCitationRule text
Galvanized steel rigid metal conduit (iron or steel product)23 CFR 635.410(b)(1)(ii)"if iron or steel products are to be used, all manufacturing processes, including application of a coating, for these materials must occur in the United States. Coating includes all processes which protect or enhance the value of the material to which the coating is applied." Galvanizing is a coating. The statute behind this is 23 U.S.C. 313.
What makes conduit an iron or steel product23 CFR 635.410(c)(1)(iii) and (vi)(iii): "articles, materials, or supplies that consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or a combination of both." (vi): predominantly means "the cost of the iron and steel content exceeds 50 percent of the total cost of all its components."
Steel conduit: small amounts of foreign steel23 CFR 635.410(b)(4)The rule does "not prevent a minimal use of foreign iron or steel products, if the cost of such materials used does not exceed one-tenth of one percent (0.1 percent) of the total contract cost or $2,500, whichever is greater", valued as delivered to the project.
HDPE conduit, and PVC conduit (construction material)2 CFR 184.6(a)(2)"Plastic and polymer-based products. All manufacturing processes, from initial combination of constituent plastic or polymer-based inputs, or, where applicable, constituent composite materials, until the item is in its final form, occurred in the United States." The requirement itself comes from the Build America, Buy America Act, section 70914 (Pub. L. 117-58).
What makes conduit a construction material2 CFR 184.3 (Construction materials)The listed items include "(ii) Plastic and polymer-based products (including polyvinylchloride, composite building materials, and polymers used in fiber optic cables)". Also: "Minor additions of articles, materials, supplies, or binding agents to a construction material do not change the categorization of the construction material."
Aluminum rigid conduit (non-ferrous metal)2 CFR 184.6(a)(1)"Non-ferrous metals. All manufacturing processes, from initial smelting or melting through final shaping, coating, and assembly, occurred in the United States."
One category per item2 CFR 184.4(e)–(f); 23 CFR 635.410(c)(2)An item "should only be classified into one" category, "based on its status at the time it is brought to the work site", and "must meet the Buy America Preference for only the single category in which it is classified." 23 CFR 635.410(c)(2) says the same for FHWA.
No 55 percent test for conduit23 CFR 635.410(c)(1)(iv)"If an item is classified as an iron or steel product, an excluded material, or other product category as specified by law or in 2 CFR part 184, then it is not a manufactured product." The Oct 1, 2026 rule (US manufacture plus more than 55 percent domestic components) applies to manufactured products only.
